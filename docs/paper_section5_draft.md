## 5.1 Data

**Legal corpus and KB.** The corpus contains 18 Vietnamese legal documents (laws, decrees and circulars on
social insurance, labour, enterprises and taxation; 106 chapters, 1,159 articles). An LLM extractor
(GPT-4o-mini, one call per article) produced 7,906 candidate rules. For each domain, the articles cited by the
benchmark questions were selected and their rules re-expressed over a shared predicate registry and reviewed,
giving five domain KBs with 636 rules in total (social insurance 149, enterprise 70, tax 200, labour 129,
cross-domain 88) and 422 encoded exception relations. Rules are Horn clauses over literals with an article-level
source; patches were derived **only from development-split failures**.

**Questions.** 300 Vietnamese application questions (social insurance 90, enterprise 70, tax 70, labour 30,
cross-domain 40), covering 225 distinct legal cases (the rest are paraphrases). Each question is annotated
with the legal goal, the case facts (with verbatim spans), the gold conclusion (yes 144 / no 85 /
insufficient 71) and its legal basis. "Insufficient" is defined by the law, independently of the KB:
(i) a missing general condition makes a case insufficient; (ii) an exception that the question does not raise
does not apply; (iii) an exception that is raised only partially makes the case insufficient. The gold labels
were generated with GPT-4o and checked against the cited provisions; an independent legal review is ongoing.

**Splits.** Questions are split by legal case (paraphrases stay together) into development (194) and
held-out test (106) sets; all social-insurance questions were used to develop the KB and are development
only. All model choices (measurement model, prompt, acceptance threshold) were made on the development set.

**Diagnostic instances.** (a) *Controlled fault injection*: from every question whose gold conclusion the KB
reproduces, single-fault instances are generated for each layer — a fact removed from the question
(Missing Case Information), removed from the parse (Parser Omission), negated (Parser Misinterpretation),
an unstated fact added (Unsupported Parser Fact), and, where two encoded rules compete, the overlap kept or
its encoded exception removed (Resolvable Rule Overlap / Unresolved KB Conflict); an instance is kept only
if the fault is observable in the reasoning (1,499 development / 451 test instances). (b) *External fault
set*: 180 F1–F4 fixtures built independently of the KB (faults chosen from the question annotation only)
plus 20 fault-free controls; 166 are usable (14 excluded because the KB does not reproduce the source
question or the source label was revised), 134 of them with an observable fault.

## 5.2 Compared systems and setup

- **GPT-4o (pure LLM)**: question only, fixed prompt, temperature 0; returns a verdict and an IRAC answer.
  No KB, no retrieval, no parsed or gold facts.
- **Symbolic QA**: GPT-4o semantic parser → domain KB → three-valued forward reasoner; no diagnosis.
- **Heuristic diagnosis**: Symbolic QA plus an IF–THEN classifier over *the same evidence* (accepted NLI
  measurements of all relevant propositions, KB verification of all competing pairs) and the same recovery.
- **Ours (cross-layer MBD)**: Symbolic QA plus consistency-based diagnosis in SWI-Prolog over user,
  parser and KB components, diagnosis-directed measurement (first-disagreement policy), clarification on
  missing-vs-omitted ambiguity, and layer-ordered recovery.

All systems use the gold legal goal (oracle-goal setting). **Measurement model.** The NLI measurement of a
proposition is the mean label distribution of mDeBERTa-v3 (XNLI) and GPT-4o-mini (logprobs of the three
option letters, one prompt, temperature 0); a label becomes an observation only if its probability is at
least τ = 0.50, otherwise the measurement yields ⊥ and adds no observation. Model, prompt and τ were chosen
on the development split only (criterion fixed in advance: mean of diagnosis accuracy and 1 − false-alarm
rate). Diagnosis search enumerates set-minimal diagnoses by increasing cardinality up to 5 faults, lowering
the bound when the candidate space exceeds 2·10⁵. In the fault-injection benchmark a simulated user
answers clarification requests with the gold case facts; in end-to-end QA no user is simulated and a
clarification request counts as the answer "insufficient".

## 5.3 Metrics

QA accuracy (verdict = gold); diagnosis accuracy (the first diagnosis is exactly the injected fault, or no
fault on controls) and per-category precision/recall/F1; **correct action** (diagnosis correct, or the only
open choice is missing-vs-omitted on the true proposition and the system asks the user about exactly it);
recovery gain (QA accuracy on faulty inputs minus that of Symbolic QA); false-alarm rate on fault-free
controls; and cost: NLI measurements and **clarification requests per query**. 95% confidence intervals are
percentile bootstrap intervals (1,000 resamples; on the external set, source questions are resampled).
**IRAC quality** is scored on 40 stratified test questions with the rubric
IRAC = 100·(0.10·I + 0.35·R + 0.40·A + 0.15·C), each component 0 / 0.5 / 1; language is not scored.
Answers are anonymised and shuffled; the judge is GPT-4.1 (not the GPT-4o baseline) and sees the gold
conclusion and legal basis; 20 questions are also scored by a human to report agreement.

**Discussion.**
(1) *Diagnosis-guided recovery is what makes the symbolic pipeline usable*: with a single fault per input,
Symbolic QA answers 4.3% of faulty inputs correctly; diagnosis and recovery raise this to 81.7% (+77.4 pp).
Replacing consistency-based diagnosis by a direct symptom-to-cause mapping loses 24.6 pp of QA accuracy and
23.7 pp of diagnosis accuracy.
(2) *MBD versus a matched IF–THEN classifier.* With identical evidence and single faults, the heuristic is
on par with or slightly above MBD in diagnosis accuracy and QA accuracy (Tables 2–3; confidence intervals
overlap). The difference is in *how* answers are obtained: the heuristic turns every unresolved condition
into a "missing information" diagnosis and asks the user about it — 3.71 questions per query, 11.8 in the
tax test set — whereas MBD asks only when the remaining diagnoses are genuinely indistinguishable
(0.35 per query, about ten times fewer). Its higher QA accuracy is thus bought with user effort that a
simulated, omniscient user hides. The main weakness of MBD is that it under-reports missing information
(recall 45.2%) and over-reports benign rule overlaps when no input fault is found.
(3) *The bottleneck is measurement, not the diagnostic logic.* With perfect measurements, MBD diagnoses 100%
of the observable external faults and 88–100% overall; with real NLI the gap is entirely explained by
measurement errors (in an error analysis on the development set with the mDeBERTa + Qwen measurement,
20 of 24 remaining errors trace back to a wrong or rejected NLI label). Better measurement translates monotonically into better diagnosis (Table 4): an open-weight
Qwen2.5-7B alone is too conservative (it labels stated facts as "not mentioned"), whereas averaging a
classifier that is precise on stated facts with an LLM that is precise on unstated ones gives the best result.
(4) *GPT-4o versus proof-grounded answers.* [GPT-4o / IRAC: QA accuracy, IRAC components; expected
pattern to check: GPT-4o writes fluent applications but cites superseded laws (R), since several
provisions in force in 2026 postdate its training data.]

**Limitations.** Gold labels are LLM-generated and still under legal review; faults are single and
injected; the simulated user knows all case facts; the legal goal is given (oracle goal); the test split
is small (106 questions), so per-domain differences are not significant.
